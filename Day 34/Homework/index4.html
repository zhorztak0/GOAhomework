Favourite Dog

Golden Retriever

🐾 Information

The Golden Retriever is a Scottish breed of retriever dog of medium-large size. It is characterised by a gentle and affectionate nature and a striking golden coat. It is a working dog, and registration is subject to successful completion of a working trial.[2] It is commonly kept as a companion dog and is among the most frequently registered breeds in several Western countries; some may compete in dog shows or obedience trials, or work as guide dogs. The Golden Retriever was bred by Sir Dudley Marjoribanks at his Scottish estate Guisachan in the late nineteenth century. He cross-bred Flat-coated Retrievers with Tweed Water Spaniels, with some further infusions of Red Setter, Labrador Retriever and Bloodhound. It was recognised by the Kennel Club in 1913, and during the interwar period spread to many parts of the world.

🐾 Interesting Facts

  1. Scottish Origins

    Developed in the mid-19th century in Scotland by Dudley Marjoribanks, also known as Lord Tweedmouth.

    Bred on the Guisachan estate to be elite hunting dogs capable of retrieving waterfowl in rainy, rugged Scottish terrain.

  2. Water-Resistant Coats

    The outer layer repels moisture while the soft undercoat provides insulation, making them exceptional natural swimmers.

    Possess a dense, water-repellent double coat.

  3. The "Soft Mouth"

    Bred with a gentle grip known as a "soft mouth".

    They can carry items—ranging from hunted game to raw eggs—in their mouths without breaking or damaging them.

  4. High Intelligence

    Ranked among the smartest dog breeds, usually sitting right behind Border Collies, Poodles, and German Shepherds.

    Their high trainability and eagerness to please make them ideal for therapy, search-and-rescue, and guide-dog work.

  5. Extended Puppyhood

    Mentally slow to mature compared to other breeds, often retaining a goofy, high-energy, puppy-like personality until they are 3 years old.

    Require consistent daily exercise and mental stimulation well past their first few years of life.
